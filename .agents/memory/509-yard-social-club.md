name: 509 Yard social-club direction
description: Visual direction and player-facing naming for 509 Yard and its games.
---

For 509 Yard, call the first game “Domino” in player-facing titles; do not use “Lakou Domino.” Reuse actual domino tile artwork for Domino marks (black-on-white or white-on-black), rather than redrawing pip patterns. Keep Word Yard and Scrabble as separate games; Scrabble has its own shared board. Box uses blue lines for Player 1 and red lines for Player 2. Filler should follow GamePigeon-style rules: randomized six-color board, opposite-corner starts, each turn chooses a color not currently used by either territory and absorbs connected tiles of that color, and most tiles wins when the board fills. Mòpyon follows Tic-Tac-Toe, then Box (Dots and Boxes-style), Kay (Mancala-style), Chess, Dame (Checkers), Word Yard, Scrabble, Barricade, Four in a Row, Filler, and Block Drop (a Tetris-style falling-block game). Yard Mode is Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe: nine small boards in a large board; a move routes the opponent to the corresponding small board, with free choice when that target is already closed. Mòpyon is five-in-a-row on intersections; marks persist and players can start anywhere. Use a 15×15 intersection board as the current adjustable default because the user was unsure of the final size. Use a restrained charcoal and black interface with a bright green game accent, white/light-gray text, and subtle borders. Keep the lobby and table low-glare, with dark surfaces and enough tile contrast for play. Keep the logo compact with clear left padding. Keep domino play and social flows central; avoid generic dark-purple game-dashboard styling, decorative scenery, and oversized promotion. Use visual references for hierarchy and mood, not literal copy. Preserve 509 Yard’s own visual character; credit only sources actually used for code, artwork, or design inspiration in About & credits. Use the supplied 509 Yard mark rather than inventing a new logo. The logo must be a transparent cutout, with no black rectangle baked into the asset or added in CSS.

**Why:** The user supplied a green-accented dashboard reference as inspiration, not a template to copy; they also asked for a smaller, padded logo, said the bright game screen hurts their eyes, requested the Domino title without “Lakou,” asked to reuse an actual Domino tile as its mark, and specified that Filler should follow GamePigeon rules.

**How to apply:** Treat references as ideas, not templates; retain the charcoal/green brand system. For credited sources, distinguish visual/gameplay inspiration from assets actually included and name the creator, source, and license when known.

**How to apply:** Carry the visual language across the lobby, auth, rules, waiting room, live table, and mobile layouts. Use “Domino” in visible titles and links; preserve legacy route aliases and internal engine identifiers unless explicitly asked to rename them.
